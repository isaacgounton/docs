# SEO - Statistics Pages Without Backlinks

> A 6-step playbook for low-authority sites to rank on "[niche] statistics/trends [year]" searches with a dated /statistics/ page built around one original number, backed by an 80-SERP study.

---

## Overview

When every target keyword is dominated by sites with thousands of referring domains, statistics and trends searches are where small sites still get onto page one. The page wins by being current and by publishing **one number nobody else can publish**, formatted so a single line can be quoted (by journalists, bloggers and AI answers). It is a link-earning asset that then feeds authority to the pages that sell.

---

## The Evidence

Observational snapshot by Borja: 80 live US searches, 10 niches (home solar to commercial real estate), all 423 page-one sites scored by referring domains. Shows who ranks now, not how fast they got there.

| Query shape | Page-one share held by sites with < 1,000 referring domains | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `[niche] statistics 2026` | 24.4% | Best opening |
| `[niche] trends 2026` | 21.4% | Good opening |
| `[niche] news` | 12.0% | Big publishers; median 16,100 RDs vs 9,476 on evergreen terms |
| `[niche] report 2026` | 10.0% | No small site in the top 3 |

- Page one for these searches averages **174 days old** vs 345 for normal keywords: freshness rotates results.
- Real examples: a dental SaaS at position 2 for "dental practice statistics 2026" with 57 referring domains; a dentist at position 5 with 59; a state stats site at position 2 for "home solar statistics 2026" with 72.
- The median site on these SERPs still has ~16,100 referring domains: page one is not weak, it just has room.

> [!warning]
> The source title promises ranking "in 24 hours", but its own study measures who ranks, not time-to-rank, and the link-earning benefit is explicitly "a bet, not a measurement". Treat speed claims as unsupported.

## The 6-Step Playbook

| # | Step | Details | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pick the keyword shape** | Target `statistics [year]` and `trends [year]`; skip `news` and `report`. Stay inside your site's topic (a dentist doing crypto stats fails). | 10 min |
| 2 | **Build `/statistics/` as a dated section** | "statistics" in the slug, an index page, a visible "last updated" date on the page, Article schema with `datePublished` and `dateModified`. | 1 h once |
| 3 | **Get a number only you have** | Your own booking, pricing or volume data ("we quoted 4,000 roofs last year"), a survey of your customers (even 60 replies), or observations from your own systems. Claude Code deep research on competitor review sentiment is one route. Borrowed stats are fine if credited, but one original number carries the page. | 1 h for the first |
| 4 | **Make each line quotable** | One stat per line, short, source named inline: "42% of X did Y in 2026, according to Z". Your own number at the top with its sample size. | 1 h |
| 5 | **Year in the title, never in the URL** | Title says 2026, URL stays `/statistics/`. Each January: swap numbers, update title year and `dateModified`, move old figures lower as history so the page accumulates age. | 30 min/year |
| 6 | **Link to money pages** | Run `site:yourdomain.com <phrase>` to avoid cannibalising your service page; link stats page to service page and back. Quarterly, check which numbers people actually quote and add more like them. | 15 min |

> [!tip]
> Nobody links to a page; they link to a number they used. A stat that only makes sense after three paragraphs never gets picked up. This is the same "standalone passage" rule used for AI citation in [[SEO - AI Search Visibility]].

> [!warning]
> Only build this if you will update it every year. A page titled 2026 still showing 2026 numbers in 2028 is worse than no page. Google's spam policies target pages that add nothing to what they copy, whether human or AI written, so a pure aggregation of others' stats is not enough.
