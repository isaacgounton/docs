# Career - Job Search Strategy

> Job search strategy that shifts effort from online applications (the crowded door) to referrals, direct outreach, and inbound (the empty doors), with a 6-week plan and a reusable AI coaching prompt.

---

## Overview

The source is an AI prompt template (GodofPrompt on X) that encodes a job search method called the "8 Empty Doors". Its core claim: mass online applying is a low-conversion commodity contest, while referrals and direct outreach to decision makers face far less competition and convert far better. The recommended move is to flip effort from roughly 90% applications / 10% alternatives to 20% / 80%.

> [!warning]
> All statistics below come from the prompt itself and are **uncited**. Some (notably "75% filtered by ATS") are widely repeated figures with weak or disputed origins. Treat the numbers as directional, not measured. The prompt's own math is also loose: 2-3 interviews per 100 applications vs 5-8 per 20 actions is roughly 10-15x per action, not the "20x" it claims.

---

## Crowded Door vs Empty Door

| | Crowded door (online applications) | Empty door (alternative approaches) |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | ~250 applicants per role, ~75 qualified | ~5 competitors |
| Gatekeeper | ATS + HR; ~6-second resume skim | Direct access to decision makers |
| Conversion (claimed) | 2-3% | Referrals 40-55%; cold outreach 15-20% |
| Example yield | 100 applications = 2-3 interviews | 20 targeted actions = 5-8 interviews |
| Contest type | Commodity: best resume wins | Differentiated: initiative wins |

The psychological framing: clicking "apply" feels like work but is "activity theater". Resumes sell the past; outreach sells the future, and companies hire for the future. The goal is not many interviews anywhere, but ~5 quality interviews at companies you actually want.

## The 8 Empty Doors

| # | Door | Action | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Referrals | Message 8-10 employees about their work (not jobs); build the relationship over 2-4 weeks | Trust transfer: an employee vouches, the hiring manager listens |
| 2 | Recruiters | "I work in X, targeting Y companies, want to be on your radar" | You bring them commission, not a favour request |
| 3 | Inbound | Post 1-2x/week about your work, projects, lessons | Expert positioning; compounds over 4-8 weeks |
| 4 | Leadership | Message founders/VPs/directors directly, skip HR, tie a result to their initiative | Decision makers bypass process for strong candidates |
| 5 | Hiring manager | "Saw the role, did similar (achievement), 15 min on your challenge?" | They want to fill the role; HR wants to filter |
| 6 | Personal brand | Be known for one thing; write and speak consistently | Top-of-mind creates inbound; 3-6 month play |
| 7 | Strategic cover letter | Name their challenge, how you solved similar, and a plan for them | Shows thinking, not just credentials |
| 8 | Combination | 5 sequenced touchpoints on one company over 4 weeks; application lands last | Familiarity bias: your name is seen 3x before the interview |

### Which doors by situation

| Situation | Priority |
|---|---|
| Zero network | Doors 4 + 5 (no existing relationships needed); ~10 messages/week |
| Career switch | Doors 3 + 7; lead with transferable achievements |
| Entry level | Door 1; projects count as achievements |
| Executive | Door 8; recruiters and CEO/founder targets |
| Burned out | One door only, halved targets; momentum first |
| Under 5 hrs/week | Door 1 only; 5 applications, 10 messages, skip content |

### Outreach message patterns

- **Networking:** specific thing about their work + your quantified result + curiosity about their challenge + "no ask here".
- **Hiring manager:** role seen + achievement with numbers + request for 15 min on their challenge.
- **Recruiter:** their specialty + your years, field, companies, targets + "keep me on your radar".

> [!tip]
> Use real, quantified achievements and specific observations about the recipient. The source's rule: generic outreach reads as desperate; specific, researched outreach reads as impressive. The same principle drives cold outreach in sales, see [[Sales - Outbound Outreach]].

## 6-Week Plan

| Weeks | Activity | Expected signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 10 applications, 20 messages, 5 priority companies, 1 post | 3-5 responses, 1-2 calls; feels slow |
| 3-4 | 3-5 conversations, 2-3 posts, 10 applications, hiring manager outreach | At least one referral in the pipeline; content getting noticed |
| 5-6 | Convergence: referrals become interviews, content brings inbound | 3-5 active processes |

> [!note]
> The source flags weeks 3-4 as where most people quit, right before the inflection point. This is the compounding argument from [[Decision Making - Principles]] applied to a job search.

### Common objections (as handled by the source)

- **No time:** 100 applications is ~20 hours for about one interview; ~10 hours of targeted outreach yields 3-5.
- **No network:** build it while searching; every conversation counts.
- **Feels desperate:** generic is desperate; specific is impressive.
- **No responses:** you need one yes. A 10% reply rate on 20 messages is 2 conversations.

## The Prompt as a Reusable Tool

The original file is a paste-into-any-chatbot prompt that turns an AI assistant into a "Job Search Strategist" coach. It is not reproduced here; the archived source holds the full text. Its structure is worth knowing if you reuse or adapt it:

- **Persona:** a former tech recruiter coaching people out of mass applying.
- **Data intake, best first:** resume upload, LinkedIn data export, LinkedIn URL, pasted headline/about/jobs, then direct questions. It extracts title, company, years, 2-3 quantified achievements, and skills.
- **Three phases:** Intake (background + 2-3 target companies), Diagnose (your application-to-interview conversion vs the benchmarks), Deliver (weekly hours and outreach comfort level, then a personalised plan).
- **Output contract:** state the 20/80 flip, pick 3 doors, write 3 personalised scripts, a week-1 list of max 5 items, the "don't quit week 3" warning, week 2 and 4 signals, under 500 words.
- **Guardrails:** separate known from inferred, never fabricate, no bracket placeholders in final scripts, cite and verify any live research.

> [!tip]
> The guardrails are the reusable part: "flag inference vs fact, no placeholders, use the user's real numbers, self-critique before sending" is a good pattern for any coaching-style prompt. Double-check the benchmark numbers it quotes back to you, since they are uncited.
