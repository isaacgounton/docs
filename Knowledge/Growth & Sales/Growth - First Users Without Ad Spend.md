# Growth - First Users Without Ad Spend

> Zero-ad-spend playbook for the first 100 paying users (Reddit value posts and DMs, competitor 1-star reviews, alternative pages, free tools, build in public) plus B2C app scaling heuristics from the Cal AI team.

---

## Overview

Early distribution follows one pattern: **go where customers already are, help before you pitch, lead with value not links, and do it every day for a month.** Running ads at $0 MRR mostly burns money. The first source is an anonymous founder's 12-channel playbook, claimed to have reached 100 paying users in under two weeks several times, with 300+ beta users from Reddit alone. The second is a set of scaling heuristics from someone who worked on growing the Cal AI app. It adds niche selection, messaging and pricing rules for B2C apps.

> [!tip]
> Don't run all 12 channels. The source's own advice: **pick 3, run them daily for a month** (or a week if you post heavily).

## The Zero-Ad-Spend Channels

### Tier 1 - Warm demand (people already stating the problem)

| Channel | How | Claimed result |
|---|---|---|
| **Reddit complaint threads** | Search subreddits for "[competitor] sucks", "looking for [tool type]", "frustrated with [problem]". DM, answer the question, mention the product naturally at the end. 10-15 unique DMs/day; follow up after 3 days | 30-40% conversion |
| **Competitor 1-2 star reviews** | Read G2, Capterra and Trustpilot reviews of every competitor. Find reviewers describing your use case, locate them on LinkedIn or X: *"saw your review about [competitor]. curious what you ended up switching to?"* | They already pay for a competitor and already said what they want |
| **Real-time complaints on X** | Google Alerts plus X monitoring for complaints ("[competitor] just lost my data", "anyone know a good [tool]?"). Reply within minutes, no link, start a conversation | The first helpful reply wins |
| **Reverse cold email** | Email ICP prospects offering something useful (a site tip, a competitor insight) that you *send after they reply*. Then: "btw I'm building [one line]. happy to show you if relevant" | Reply rate 2% → 15-20% |

### Tier 2 - Content and community

- **Reddit value posts.** You don't need an audience. About 80% of the post is pure value, with the product mentioned between the first and last paragraph (the source also suggests including at least two other links). Formats: "I built X and here's what I learned", "how to solve [problem] step by step", "I analyzed [data] and here's what I found". Post to 3-5 relevant subreddits. Never post "check out my product", which gets you banned.
- **Your own subreddit** about the problem space, not the product name. Post consistently, invite interested people from other subs, and cross-post the best content back. It becomes a community you control, free of algorithm changes.
- **Content recycling ladder:** a Reddit post that works → X thread → LinkedIn post → short-video script. One piece becomes four. Cadence: X 3-5x/day, LinkedIn 1x/day. Reply usefully to big niche accounts without pitching. Screenshots of Reddit threads with your take are named as the highest-engagement X format.
- **Communities (5-10):** Slack, Discord, Facebook groups, Indie Hackers, forums, even WhatsApp groups. Lurk first, answer questions, become a known name, then mention your product when relevant. One good Slack community can bring 20-30 users.
- **Build in public daily on X:** failures, losses, wins, revenue milestones. Put your face on everything, because people trust people, not logos. Consistency beats quality: a mediocre post every day beats a perfect one weekly.

### Tier 3 - Compounding assets

- **Alternative and comparison pages first:** "[competitor] alternative" and "[you] vs [competitor]". Honest, with pricing breakdowns, feature tables and screenshots, updated monthly. Every visitor has buying intent, so this is called the highest-ROI SEO. See [[SEO - SaaS Page System with Claude]].
- **"Boring" blog posts:** answer the exact questions from AnswerThePublic and Google's "People also ask". Long-form, mention the product only in the last paragraph. One a week for 6 months compounds into steady traffic.
- **A free tiny tool** (calculator, checker, analyzer, template) with no signup. It is found through Google and leads people to the main product.
- **Product Hunt and directories:** a spike, not a strategy. The value is the backlink and the badge. Line up your community's support before launch day and use the momentum for press and newsletters. Most launch traffic churns within 48 hours.

### Always - Do things that don't scale

Onboard every early user by hand with a demo, send a personal welcome from your real email, ask what they're trying to do, help them set it up, and check in after 3 days. Your first 100 users should feel like they know you, and they become the ones who spread the word.

## B2C App Scaling Heuristics (Cal AI)

### Niche selection - four upside predictors

1. An expanding market
2. **Cult-like audiences.** Called the single most important B2C niche factor, because of how well they convert
3. Cheap creators to partner with
4. Existing demand

### Marketing

- **Influencers:** sign mid-tier creators only at first (10-100k followers with good engagement). Household names and nano-creators are both overpriced, for different reasons.
- **Sell outcomes, not features:** Cal AI sold "the body of your dreams", not calorie tracking.
- **A magic moment:** apps that blew up had one in their marketing. If you can't state yours in one sentence, your problem isn't budget.
- UGC and slideshows take more skill to run profitably than influencers and paid ads.
- Copy frameworks, not tactics. Adapt the case study to your own app.

### Product and pricing

- **No free trial** until you've proven people will pay from the start.
- Most successful apps are entertainment disguised as productivity or education, so gamify so people want to come back.
- **Avoid lifetime plans:** possible legal headaches, complications in an acquisition, and little extra revenue over annual plans.
- **Price for mindshare first, margin second,** to become the default in the niche.
- Do as much as possible of the few tasks that actually generate revenue. A strong designer is an asset you won't regret hiring.

## Tensions and Caveats

> [!warning]
> - **Links in posts:** the Reddit advice says to include the product plus two more links in value posts, but the playbook's own summary says "lead with value not links". [[Sales - Outbound Outreach]] also claims external URLs in a first LinkedIn message cut reach by 25-40%. Default to few or no links in a first touch.
> - **Community lurking period:** the source says both "lurk for 2 weeks" and "after 2-3 days of being helpful, mention what you're building". Respect each community's self-promotion rules either way.
> - **DMs at 10-15/day** from one account can trigger platform spam limits. The GTM source recommends manual, capped sending (see [[GTM - Agent-Driven GTM Machine]]).
