## 1\. Alternative pages

Alternative to X. Free alternative to X. Open source alternative to X. Cheaper alternative to X. Alternative to X for agencies.

Highest intent on this list. Someone typing "alternative to Mangools" has already decided to leave Mangools. They are not researching a category, they are shopping for a replacement today.

And the incumbent cannot compete for it. No company writes a page helping you leave them. So the results are left to directories and to whoever bothers to show up.

Write these first. **Example:** [https://chatseo.app/blog/ahrefs-alternative](https://chatseo.app/blog/ahrefs-alternative)

## 2\. Comparison pages

X vs Y. X or Y. X vs Y vs Z. X pricing vs Y pricing. X features vs Y.

Two vendors and a pile of affiliate content. That is the entire competitive field.

Write yours and be honest about the rows where you lose. The reader already knows your page is biased. Admitting a weakness is the only thing that makes the rest of the page believable, and it is the difference between a comparison that converts and one that gets closed in four seconds. **Example:** [https://chatseo.app/blog/se-ranking-vs-serpstat](https://chatseo.app/blog/se-ranking-vs-serpstat)

## 3\. Integration pages

Tools + Slack. + Stripe. + Shopify. + Zapier. + Gmail. + WordPress.

The cheapest wins on the list. You borrow the search volume of somebody else's brand and almost nobody is fighting you for it.

Twelve integrations is twelve pages, and each one is genuinely useful rather than padded. Do these while you are still small.

## 4\. Constraint pages

Tools without subscription. Without commitment. Without credit card. Without installation. No-code tools. Open source tools.

These work because they are binary. "Tools without credit card" is a filter, not an opinion.

If you actually match the constraint, you win the page outright and there is no argument to be had. If you do not match it, do not write it.

## 5\. Pricing pages

X pricing. Free tools. Affordable tools. Tools under 50 €. Pricing per user. Free vs paid.

People search price before they search product. High intent, but directories and review sites are already sitting on most of these.

Worth doing after the first four have earned you some authority.

## 6\. Use case pages

Tools to generate leads. To manage prospects. To automate prospecting. To handle follow-ups. To retain customers.

This is where you rank for the job instead of the category. It is also where you start competing with category leaders.

Slower to land. Worth the wait.

**Example:** [https://chatseo.app/features/keyword-research-tool](https://chatseo.app/features/keyword-research-tool)

## 7\. Problem pages

Tools to automate X. To reduce X. To save time on X. To avoid X. To simplify X.

The reader does not know your category exists yet. That is the point, and it is why these are the long game.

Weak competition, low intent, real payoff at month six.

## 8\. Feature pages

Tools with API. With AI. With reporting. With automation. With a mobile app.

Only write these for features you genuinely lead on.

"Tools with reporting" is not a page if your reporting is table stakes. It is a page if your reporting is the reason people switch to you. Otherwise you are publishing a directory listing about yourself.

**Example:** [https://chatseo.app/features/seo-mcp](https://chatseo.app/features/seo-mcp)

## 9\. Company size pages

Tools for freelancers. For micro-enterprises. For SMEs. For mid-sized companies. For teams of 5.

I would mostly skip these.

They are almost always the same page with three words swapped. Google has a name for that pattern and it is not a compliment. If your product genuinely works differently for a freelancer than for a 200-person company, write it. If the only difference is the H1, you are building doorway pages.
