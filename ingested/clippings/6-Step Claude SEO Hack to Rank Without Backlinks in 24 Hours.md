## The 6-step playbook

## Step 1: Pick the type of keyword

Not every search is equally hard. I checked, and the gap is big.

↳ Go after \<your niche> statistics 2026 and \<your niche> trends 2026

↳ Skip \<your niche> news and \<your niche> report 2026

On statistics searches, 24% of page one goes to sites with under 1,000 links. On trends, 21%. On news it drops to 12%. On report searches, 10%, and no small site made the top three at all. News is where the big publishers live. Statistics is where you can win.

Stick to what your site is already about. A dentist doing crypto statistics is the one that fails.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAVJhmWsAEyG_Z?format=jpg&name=large)

Time: 10 minutes.

## Step 2: Build /statistics as its own dated section

Make sure "statistics" is in the Slug of the URL

↳ Put it at /statistics/, with an index page

↳ Show the date you last updated it, on the page, not just in the code

↳ Use Article schema with datePublished and dateModified

Page one for these searches is 174 days old, against 345 for normal keywords. Old pages do not sit there. A visible update date is how you stay in the rotation.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAVixsW0AEvWRI?format=jpg&name=large)

Time: 1 hour once.

## Step 3: Get a number only you have

This is the whole thing. Google punishes pages that add nothing to what they copied, and it does not matter if a human or an AI wrote it. A page of other people's stats is a copy.

So publish something only you can count:

↳ Your own booking, pricing or volume data: "we quoted 4,000 roofs last year, here is what they cost"

↳ A survey of your own customers, even 60 replies

↳ What you see that the trade press cannot, from your own systems

One original number carries the page. The rest can be sourced from others, as long as you credit them.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAVqJDWQAIEg0W?format=jpg&name=large)

**Pro tip:** Ask Claude Code to run Deep Research and find unique insights no other website has. Analyze the sentiment in reviews of your competitors or other companies in your Industry, for example. Time: 1 hour for the first one.

## Step 4: Write it so one line can be quoted

Nobody links to a page. They link to a number they used on their content.

↳ One stat per line, short, with the source named right there

↳ Make every line stand on its own: "42% of X did Y in 2026, according to Z"

↳ Put your own number at the top, above the borrowed ones

↳ Add the sample size next to it, so a journalist can trust it

A stat that only makes sense after three paragraphs never gets picked up.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAVuL5WAAAyfRB?format=jpg&name=large)

Time: 1 hour.

## Step 5: Put the year in the title, never in the URL

You want the page to look current every January without starting over.

↳ Title says 2026. URL stays /statistics/ with no year in it

↳ Every January, swap the numbers, change the title year, update dateModified

↳ Keep the old figures further down as history, so the page grows instead of resetting

Put 2026 in the URL and you throw away the page's age every year, which is the one thing you cannot buy.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAVx5hWwAAmZqR?format=jpg&name=large)

Time: 30 minutes a year.

## Step 6: Link it to your money pages

The stats page will get you the clicks but from there you need to drive visitors (and SEO link juice) to your money pages

↳ First search site:[yourdomain.com](https://yourdomain.com/) \<your phrase> so you do not compete with your own service page

↳ Link the stats page to that service page

↳ Link the service page back to the stats page

↳ Check every quarter which numbers people actually quoted, and add more of those

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HQAV1pmXUAEhR_V?format=jpg&name=large)

Time: 15 minutes.

## Two things to know first

Do not build this unless you will update it every year. A stats page with 2026 in the title, still showing 2026 numbers in 2028, is worse than no page. The date you were using as an advantage becomes the reason people leave.

The link part is a bet, not a measurement. I proved small sites rank for these searches. I did not measure how many links they earned. The reasoning is simple. People cite numbers, and your own number is the only one nobody else can publish. Treat that as a reason to try, not a promise.

## The study behind this

> I analyzed 80 live US Google searches across ten niches, from home solar to commercial real estate. Then I scored all 423 sites that held a page-one spot, using their referring domain counts. On \<niche> statistics 2026, 24.4% of page one went to sites with under 1,000 referring domains. On \<niche> trends 2026 it was 21.4%. On \<niche> news it was 12.0%, and on \<niche> report 2026 just 10.0%, where no small site reached the top three. The middle site on these searches has 16,100 referring domains, so page one is not weak. It just has room in it, and that room sits on the statistics and trends searches. This is one snapshot of one day, and it shows who ranks now, not how quickly anyone got there.

Real examples from page one: [patientfy.ai](https://patientfy.ai/) at position 2 for dental practice statistics 2026 with 57 referring domains. [premierimagedentistry.com](https://premierimagedentistry.com/) at position 5 on the same search with 59. [docsels.com](https://docsels.com/) at position 7 for dental practice trends 2026 with 50. [californiadgstats.ca.gov](https://californiadgstats.ca.gov/) at position 2 for home solar statistics 2026 with 72.

I also checked whether news searches are easier than normal keywords. They are not. Sites on news page one have a median of 16,100 referring domains against 9,476 on evergreen searches, so news is the harder route, not the easier one. That is why this playbook targets statistics instead.
